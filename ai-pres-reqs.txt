**Yes, it will make a solid portfolio piece**, provided you frame it properly in your repository's Readme.

In tech portfolios, recruiters and hiring managers aren't looking for a "perfect" price prediction—everyone knows predicting asset prices with classical time series models is notoriously difficult. What they care about is **how you explain your technical choices and document your engineering process**.

**What Makes It a Good Portfolio Piece**

* **Clear Problem & Baseline:** Setting up a classic statistical model (ARIMA) as a performance baseline demonstrates a solid understanding of fundamental time series concepts before jumping to complex machine learning architectures like LSTMs or Transformers.
* **Justified Model Choice:** Explaining *why* you chose ARIMA over SARIMA (identifying lack of seasonality) or Exponential Smoothing shows you can interpret data visually and theoretically, rather than blindly training algorithms.
* **Clean Workflow:** Showing end-to-end steps—from data processing and visualization to evaluation metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE)—reflects a structured analytics workflow.

**How to Make It Stand Out on GitHub**

To maximize its impact without adding heavy scope, focus on presentation:

* **Structure the Readme:** Lead with a quick summary, your key visualization, the justification for choosing ARIMA, and the final error metrics.
* **Document the Limitations:** Add a short "Key Takeaways" or "Limitations" section. Briefly mentioning that linear models struggle with extreme crypto volatility shows strong domain awareness.
* **Include Code Hygiene:** Keep your code modular, well-commented, and provide a clear `requirements.txt` so anyone can easily replicate your results.
